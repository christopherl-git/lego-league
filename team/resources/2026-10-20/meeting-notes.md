### Alexis: brown tree snake in Guam

The brown tree snake is killing native animals in Guam. Alexis said native mammals. The USGS fact sheet also covers what the snake has done to Guam's native forest birds, so check what it says.

**Already tried:**

- Dead mice with poison in them, used as bait
- Snake-sniffing dogs
- Water testing

The snakes are still in Guam.

**Sources:** U.S. Geological Survey (USGS) fact sheet; USDA National Wildlife Research Center

**Solution ideas:**

- Trap the snakes, or build a better trap
- Keep other animals out of the trap
- Use a camera that recognizes the snake and opens a door

**What the group found:** snake traps already exist, and they use a one-way door (source: University of Washington). A camera would cost more and work less well than the one-way door.

### Ira: dog-strangling vine in Ontario

Dog-strangling vine (DSV) kills native plants. Ira had no sources yet. The group found sources in its own research (see below).

**Already tried:**

- Chemicals kill it, but they also kill other plants
- Digging it up works, but it is hard work
- Goats are used to eat the vine, grazing only in a fenced-off area

**What the group found:**

- DSV grows in thick mats that choke out native plants. It is a problem in Ontario and southern Quebec. It comes from Europe. (Invasive Species Centre, .ca; Ontario Invasive Plant Council, .ca)
- Its roots release a chemical that kills other plants (watersheds.ca)
- It is toxic to humans. The plant has to be dried out to kill it. (PEI Invasive Species Council, peiinvasives.com)
- To remove it you have to dig out the whole deep root without snapping the stem. Don't put it in the garbage or compost, where it will regrow. Put it in a thick garbage bag. (Ottawa Field-Naturalists' Club, .ca)
- There are chemical options for killing it

**Solution ideas:**

- A robot digger that removes the whole plant
- Block its sunlight: drones fly boxes over the plants to cover them

**Discussion:** DSV isn't a rainforest problem (this season's theme is biodiversity in the rainforest), and we don't have a good solution for it yet.
