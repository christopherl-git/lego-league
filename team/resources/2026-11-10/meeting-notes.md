## Innovation project: our solution

**Our solution: map where the invasive grass grows, then send a winged drone.** Satellite images show where the grass is. Winged drones fly there to find fires early and drop water on them before they spread. This combines idea F (map, then send a drone) with idea D (winged drones).

Everyone scored each idea from 1 to 5 on three things: **new**, **doable** and **helps biodiversity** (out of 15 each, 135 for the team). Worksheet: [solution-scoring-worksheet.pdf](../../../innovation_project/solution-scoring-worksheet/solution-scoring-worksheet.pdf). Scan of the team scores: [team-scores-2026-11-10.pdf](../../../innovation_project/solution-scoring-worksheet/team-scores-2026-11-10.pdf).

Each person's scores are in the [project plan](../../../innovation_project/project-plan.md#solution-chosen-2026-11-10). The sheet gives sand drones 86, but their scores add up to 72. The order is the same either way.

**What the team wrote:**
- We chose it because "we think it's newer and better."
- It beat D because "it is D and F combined."
- Still to prove: "we need to prove that it is possible."
- What we build or draw on 11-17: not decided yet.

Judges will want more than "newer and better." Facts we already have that help:
- **New:** the fixes that already exist (herbicide, planting native trees, stopping loans for clearing land) don't spot the grass early or fight its fires (Alexis, 11-03).
- **Doable:** satellite pictures from NASA Landsat are free (Sylvie, Amelia). We still need a source on how far a winged drone can fly and how much it can carry.
- **Helps biodiversity:** stopping fires early breaks the cycle where fire helps the grass spread (Fusco and others, 2021, found by Sylvie and Amelia).
