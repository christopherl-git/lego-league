Source: [fll-founders-challenge-bioglow-EN.pdf](fll-founders-challenge-bioglow-EN.pdf), the official FIRST LEGO League Challenge **Engineering Notebook** for the **FIRST CANOPY presented by Qualcomm** season and the **BIOGLOW** challenge (32 pages). It is a team guide, not required at judging, but it is a good thing to bring to the judging event and the sessions below give us a ready-made season plan. Page numbers below are the notebook's own.

## The big picture

- FLL Challenge is scored in **four equally weighted parts, 25% each**: **Core Values**, **Innovation Project**, **Robot Design**, and **Robot Game**.
- At the event we give judges two **5-minute** presentations (innovation project and robot design). The robot is scored by referees in the **2.5-minute** robot game, with multiple matches. **Only the highest score counts.**
- The season theme is **biodiversity in the rainforest**. Robot missions mimic seed dispersal, fungal networks, canopy research, habitat protection and species conservation.
- Throughout the season we use the **Engineering Design Process**: **Identify, Design, Create, Iterate, Communicate**. Judges want evidence of each step, especially what we changed and why.

## Innovation project (pages 5 to 6)

The challenge: **identify a problem that puts biodiversity at risk and design an innovative solution that can help.**

What judges look for, by Design Process step:

1. **Identify:** clearly define the problem and why it matters. Research it and the solutions that already exist, using reliable sources.
2. **Design:** brainstorm (new idea, adapt technology from another field, or improve an existing one). Outline a project plan.
3. **Create:** describe how the solution addresses the problem and how it affects biodiversity. Make a **prototype model or drawing**. It doesn't need to work, it just has to help explain the solution.
4. **Iterate:** share the idea with other teams or mentors, collect feedback, record what we learned and what we changed. Show evidence of improvements.
5. **Communicate:** a five-minute live presentation. Use the rubric as the guide. Every team member participates, and we highlight Core Values.

**Project Sparks** (starter ideas, or choose our own problem): drones and aerial technology; ecologists' simple tools for studying how rainforest plants grow and support wildlife; mapping technology to restore land for native plants and animals; wildlife biologists' camera traps and tracking devices. The rainforest layers shown are forest floor, understory, canopy, and emergent.

**Where our topic fits:** we chose **invasive species** (2026-10-06), which harms native species and reduces biodiversity. That fits the "problem that puts biodiversity at risk" prompt. Our problem statement should explain **why the problem exists, why it's important to fix, and who would be affected if it were solved** (session 3 tip).

## Robot design and robot game (page 4)

- **Identify:** pick which missions to attempt and in what order (a mission strategy). Explore building and coding resources.
- **Design:** every team member contributes ideas for building, coding and strategy. **Document how the robot design changes** as we learn.
- **Create:** explain the purpose of each design or attachment. Show how code and attachments support the mission strategy.
- **Iterate:** **test repeatedly on the game field and record what works, what doesn't, and what we adjust.** Use test evidence to improve consistency, accuracy and mission performance.
- **Communicate:** five minutes on the robot design process. Plan to talk about it **without the game field**.

This is why our [robot design log](../../../robot_design/design-log.md) and the Kp/Ki tuning record matter: judges ask what changed and what evidence led to it.
