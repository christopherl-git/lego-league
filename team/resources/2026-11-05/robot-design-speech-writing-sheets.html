At judging we give a 5-minute robot design explanation with no game field, then judges ask questions for 5 more minutes. These pages guide you through each part of the speech. They give you sentence starters, not the answers. You write what we really did, so it sounds like us.

What to do

  1. Read the gold box on each page. It tells you what judges listen for.
  2. Write an answer under each ✎ starter. Use the reference pages (meeting notes, design log, test results) to find facts.
  3. Add numbers. How many runs? How far off? How many out of 10?
  4. Don't know? Write [ASK: name] and we will track it down.
  5. Practice out loud. About 600 to 700 words is 5 minutes. Speak slowly.

What makes a speech score high?

  • Say why. "We changed it because ..." beats "We changed it."
  • Say what is new. Tell the judges what we changed from the original build.
  • Tell the test story: we tested, found a problem, changed it, tested again, it got better.
  • Name everyone. All nine of us speak and all nine of us did real work.
  • Be proud. Smile. Celebrate what we did.

The robot design process we follow

Identify → Design → Create → Iterate (test and improve) → Communicate what we learned. The seven speech sections follow this order.

Questions judges may ask

Jot a short answer or the facts you will use.

1. Why did you choose this base robot?
2. What is the most important change you made, and why?
3. How do you know your robot is accurate?
4. Walk us through how your code works.
5. What did you do when something did not work?
6. Who built each attachment? Who wrote the code?
7. How did you choose which attachment for each mission?
8. What would you change with more time?
