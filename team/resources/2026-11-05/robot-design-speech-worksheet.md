# Part 1: Speech template

## How judging works (read this first)

| Step | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Robot Design: Team Explanation | **5 minutes** | **This speech.** We talk, judges listen. No game field. |
| Robot Design: Question and Answer | 5 minutes | Judges ask about what we said. |

Five minutes is about **600 to 700 spoken words**. Speak slowly. Judges score only what they **hear**.

**Judges tick one box per row.** To be marked **Accomplished (3)** we need *clear evidence*. To be marked **Exceeds (4)** the judges must write down how we exceeded. Give them something to write.

| Rubric area | What judges listen for at **Accomplished** | How to give them more (**Exceeds**) |
|---|---|---|
| **IDENTIFY** | Clear mission strategy. Clear use of building and coding resources. | Say *why* we chose missions in that order. Name the specific resources and what we took from each. Say who we asked for help. |
| **DESIGN** | *All* team members contributed ideas. Building and coding skills in *all* members. | Name **every** teammate and one thing each did. Show that people learned a new skill (building *and* coding). |
| **CREATE** | Clear explanation of innovative attachments and their purpose. Clear explanation of innovative code and sensor use. | Say what we **changed from the original** and why. Explain the code step by step. Say what each sensor does for us. |
| **ITERATE** | Clear evidence of **repeated** testing. Clear evidence of improvements **based on testing**. | Use **numbers** (how many runs, how far off). Tell the story: *we tested, we found a problem, we changed this, we tested again, it got better.* |
| **COMMUNICATE** | Detailed explanation of the process and lessons learned. Clear pride and enthusiasm. | Say what we would tell another team. Smile, pace yourselves, and celebrate what we are proud of. |

**Core Values count too.** Teamwork, discovery, inclusion, innovation, impact and fun are all judged. Slip them in naturally (there is a prompt in section 7).

**Rules for writing your part**

1. Every blank needs a **fact from Part 2**. If you can't find it, write **[ASK: who]** and we will track it down.
2. Use **numbers** wherever you can ("10 runs", "within 2 mm"), not "pretty good" or "a lot".
3. Always answer **why**. A choice with no reason scores low.
4. Write it the way you talk. Short sentences. Read it out loud once you are done.
5. Don't read a list. Tell the story.

### Key facts by decision

**Front support (entries 3, 5, 9).** Three versions. (1) Original wheels have no rubber, so they skid or drag differently from run to run. (2) Mine cart wheels: lower profile, less friction when turning, very good in a straight line, but resisted when turning and stuck out past the sides, so a wheel could touch a wall before the body. (3) **Technic ball joint**: better for turns, stays under the robot, sides are clear for squaring. It added **wobble**, which Jordan fixed by retuning Kp and Ki. Guidance followed: smaller wheels wobble less and accuracy matters more than speed. Known risk: a caster can drift on hard acceleration and when reversing (**"it is being fixed"**).

**Second color sensor (entry 4).** Squaring on a line needs two sensors: when one sees black, the robot turns until the other also sees black, then wiggles until straight. Without squaring it drove crooked, and with squaring it didn't (our worksheet answer). Why it matters: setup mistakes at the table stop costing points once the robot straightens itself on a line. The square-up program was finished 2026-10-01.

**Gyro blocks (entry 8).**

- **`Gyro Move`** drives straight by correcting heading error every 0.1 seconds: proportional (Kp) plus integral (Ki), with an acceleration ramp (start at 15, speed up by 5, slow down by 8 at 70% of the distance).

- **`Gyro Turn`** turns to an angle at a speed that falls as the robot nears the target (25 to 90 power), and stops 3 degrees early so it doesn't overshoot.

- Both are reusable My Blocks, so any mission program can call them with a distance, a speed or an angle.

- Why yaw: yaw turns the robot by a real angle, while 90 motor degrees only turns the wheel a quarter turn.

**Distance in cm (entry 10).** The robot drove a measured 100 cm (1 m measuring stick) and that took 2070 motor degrees, so **20.7 degrees per cm**. About 5.75 wheel turns. Measuring on the table is better than calculating from the wheel size, because it includes tire squash and slip.

**Attachment pins (entry 7).** Jack suggested axle pins because it would be **faster to put on and off**. The lifting arm kept coming off, so we went back to **quick pins, which hold more securely**. A secure fit matters more than swap speed, because an attachment that falls off mid-run loses points.

**Attachment swap speed.** Our review put HummerOne's full-width bar at about 5 seconds to swap versus 20 seconds for a pinned design.

**What it shows**

- **Kp 2 with Ki 1 is the best pair we tested.** Three runs ended within 1.6 mm of straight, versus about 9.6 mm for the old Kp 2.1 and Ki 2.

- **Low Kp drifts, high Kp weaves.** Kp 1.0 and 1.5 ended 19 to 29 mm off to the right. Kp 3.0 had a wobble score of 3 on one run.

- **The data is noisy.** Some values had runs of 17 to 20 mm next to runs near 0. Three runs per value is not enough to rank them closely. The 10-run confirmation at Kp 2 and Ki 1 is still to do.

- **Nearly every miss was to the right.** That could mean a steady pull right on the bare robot. Check for a wheel or ball joint problem.
