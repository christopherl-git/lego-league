## Observations before voting

- **Sources:** three topics (Alexis, Amelia, Sylvie) came with sources. Ella, Brynn and Jack still need one. This was the assignment from 2026-09-29, so the unsourced topics are not yet proven problems. A topic can still win the vote, but its source has to be found before we commit.
- **Overlap:** Ella's pollution is very broad and overlaps with Alexis (ocean acidification is caused by carbon pollution) and Brynn (light pollution is a type of pollution). If Ella's topic wins, it needs to be narrowed to one specific kind of pollution.
- **Common thread:** almost every topic ends in habitat loss or reduced biodiversity. That is a good theme to build the project around, whichever topic wins.
- **Source quality:** Alexis's NOAA source is a government science agency. Sylvie's CBC source is a news outlet, so look for a science or government source behind it too (for example Natural Resources Canada). Amelia's sources are advocacy groups, which is fine, but judges may ask for a scientific study as well.
- **Which problem is the strongest?** These are the questions to ask for each topic:
  - Is it a real, proven problem with a credible source?
  - Can we design a solution (a device, program, or change in behaviour) and not just describe the problem?
  - Can we talk to a real expert or someone affected, and test an idea ourselves?
  - Is it near us (Toronto or Canada), so we can see it or visit it?
  - Does the whole team care about it enough to work on it for months?

## How to vote

1. **Clarifying questions (5 min):** anyone can ask a presenter one question. No debating yet.
2. **Score each topic (written, secret):** every member gives their top three choices: **3 points** to first choice, **2** to second, **1** to third. Members may vote for their own topic.
3. **Count the points.** The highest total wins.
4. **Tie-break:** if the top two are within 1 point, do a quick runoff: a show of hands between just those two.
5. **If the winner has no source yet,** the presenter and one partner find one by next meeting. If they can't, the second-place topic takes over.
