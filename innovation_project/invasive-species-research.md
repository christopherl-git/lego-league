## Brown tree snake (Guam)

- **Problem:** kills native animals in Guam
- **Already tried:** bait mice with poison in them, snake-sniffing dogs, water testing. The snakes are still there.
- **Our ideas:** a better trap; keep other animals out of it; a camera that recognizes the snake and opens a door
- **What we found:** snake traps already exist and use a one-way door. A camera would cost more and work less well.
- **Sources:** USGS fact sheet; USDA National Wildlife Research Center; University of Washington (traps)

## Dog-strangling vine (Ontario)

- **Problem:** grows in thick mats that choke out native plants, and its roots release a chemical that kills other plants. Native to Europe. Toxic to humans.
- **Already tried:** chemicals (also kill other plants); digging (works but hard); goats grazing in a fenced-off area
- **How to remove it:** dig out the whole deep root without snapping the stem. Don't put it in the garbage or compost, where it regrows. Bag it in a thick garbage bag. It has to be dried out to die.
- **Our ideas:** a robot digger; drones that place boxes over the plants to block their sunlight
- **Sources:** Invasive Species Centre (.ca); Ontario Invasive Plant Council (.ca); Ottawa Field-Naturalists' Club (.ca); watersheds.ca; PEI Invasive Species Council (peiinvasives.com)

## Chosen problem: invasive grass and rainforest fires (2026-10-27)

- **Problem:** grass dries up and causes fires, and the fires let more grass take over, so it turns into a cycle (Jordan). Roads make room for invasive grass to spread into rainforests.
- **Our ideas:** drones that put out fires and also detect them; drones that drop sand; map and monitor the invasive grass; herbicide on the grass beside roads
- **Sources:** Sylvie's two YouTube videos on drones carrying liquid-filled balls to put out fires (see [2026-10-27 notes](../team/resources/2026-10-27/meeting-notes.md)). Still needed: credible written sources for the grass–fire cycle.

### What already works and what doesn't (Alexis, 2026-11-03)

From Alexis's "Rainforest Grass" document:

| Approach | Result |
|---|---|
| Special herbicides that kill the grass without hurting the trees | Works |
| Plant native trees so their leaves block the sunlight the grass needs | Works |
| Government stops giving loans to farmers who clear rainforest for cattle pasture | Works |
| Bird perches so birds drop seeds that crowd out the grass | Kind of works |
| Cutting the grass by machine or by hand | Doesn't work |
| Letting the grass die by itself | Doesn't work |
| Mild grass herbicides | Doesn't work |

None of these is about detecting grass or fighting the fires, which is where our drone ideas fit.

### New solution idea (Sylvie, 2026-11-03)

Use jet or winged (fixed-wing) drones instead of copter drones to find and fight fires. They can fly farther and longer, which matters over large rainforest areas. Question to answer: can a fixed-wing drone carry and drop fire-fighting material, and how would it be launched and recovered?

### Finding a source for the grass–fire cycle (leads, 2026-11-03)

**Found (2026-11-03, Sylvie and Amelia):** Fusco, Balch, Mahood, Nagy and others, "The human–grass–fire cycle: how people and invasives co-occur to drive fire regimes," Frontiers in Ecology and the Environment, 2021 (esajournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/fee.2432). The abstract says invasive grasses alter fire regimes and create a self-reinforcing cycle of more fire and more flammable grass, and that people introduce the grasses (farming), spread them (roads) and cause ignitions. This answers the cycle claim and supports our roads point. Read the full paper for numbers.

**Search terms** (Google Scholar, or the library): "grass-fire cycle", "invasive grass fire regime", "exotic grass fire tropical forest", "gamba grass fire Australia", "Guinea grass fire", "cogon grass fire", "molasses grass fire", "invasive grass wildfire rainforest edge". Add the grass name to each.

**Papers (found by title lookup; read the abstract before using):**
- D'Antonio and Vitousek, "Biological Invasions by Exotic Grasses, the Grass/Fire Cycle, and Global Change," Annual Review of Ecology and Systematics, 1992. The paper that named the cycle. Identity confirmed.
- Rossiter-Rachor, Setterfield and others, "Andropogon gayanus (Gamba Grass) Invasion Increases Fire-mediated Nitrogen Losses in the Tropical Savannas of Northern Australia," Ecosystems, 2007. Identity confirmed; covers gamba grass and fire.
- Not confirmed, search by title: Williams and Baruch, "African grass invasion in the Americas: ecosystem consequences and the role of ecophysiology" (Biological Invasions, 2000, covers Guinea grass); Veldman and Putz on grass-dominated vegetation and fires in the Amazon.

**Sites likely to say it plainly (check each page):**
- Invasive Species Compendium (CABI, cabidigitallibrary.org/journal/cabicompendium): search each grass; the "Impact" section often describes fire.
- Global Invasive Species Database (iucngisd.org): Guinea grass and Gamba grass pages.
- Australian Government and Northern Territory gamba grass pages (search "Gamba grass NT Government").
- USDA Forest Service Fire Effects Information System (fs.usda.gov/database/feis): cogon grass and Guinea grass summaries.
- Hawaii Wildfire Management Organization (hawaiiwildfire.org): fire and invasive grass in Hawaii.
- The Science (AAAS) and Independent articles Amelia found: re-read them for a line on fire, then follow their cited study.

**Tips:** a source is strongest when it is a journal paper, government page or university page, and it says in one sentence that the grass makes fires more likely or more intense, and that fire helps the grass spread. Open the paper's abstract on the publisher page and, if a site blocks access, use Google Scholar or the library, or ask a coach.
