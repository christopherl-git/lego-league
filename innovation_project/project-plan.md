## Solution options (to choose on 2026-11-10)

| Idea | Why it might win | Question to answer |
|---|---|---|
| Drones that detect fires and put them out | Matches Sylvie's sources; judges like adapting existing technology | Do rainforest fire drones already exist? What would we do that is new? |
| Drones that drop sand | Simple to explain and prototype | Does sand put out grass fires? Is it safe for the soil? |
| Map and monitor the invasive grass | Stops the cycle before fires start | What mapping already exists? How would we spot grass early? |
| Winged (fixed-wing) drones to find and fight fires (Sylvie, 11-03) | Fly farther and longer than copter drones, so they cover big rainforest areas | Can a winged drone drop fire-fighting material? How is it launched and landed? |
| Herbicide beside roads | Roads let grass spread in | Harms native plants; not very innovative |

Alexis's research (11-03) shows herbicide, planting native trees and cutting off cattle-pasture loans already work. Nobody there detects the grass early or fights the fires, so a detection and fire-response idea fits a real gap.

My lean is detection plus response: map where the grass grows and send a drone to the places that are about to burn. That answers the whole cycle, not one step.

## New sources to find (to verify the project)

The sources we have prove the grass is a problem and show what already works. They don't yet prove the two ideas behind our solution, so look for these:

| Claim to prove | What to look for | Who | Good places |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grass makes fires worse, and fires spread the grass (the cycle) | One source from a government, university or conservation group that says it directly, with numbers (how much hotter or bigger the fires are) | Found: Fusco and others 2021 (Sylvie, Amelia) | Science articles above; CSIRO or Australian Government (Gamba grass); USDA or U.S. Forest Service (Guinea, Cogon); Hawaii Wildfire Management Organization |
| Fires harm biodiversity | A source on what animals and plants are lost when rainforest burns | Alexis | Conservation groups (WWF, IUCN), a university |
| Early detection of the grass works, and mapping is feasible | Drone or satellite mapping of invasive plants, and how accurate it is | Sylvie, Amelia | University or government remote-sensing studies; search "drone mapping invasive grass" |
| Drones can find and fight fires | Written (not YouTube) source on drones detecting or putting out wildfires, and what limits them (weight, battery, wind) | Fedor, Ira, Jordan, Jack | Fire agencies; Natural Resources Canada; research articles on wildfire drones |
| Winged drones fly farther and longer than copters | Range, endurance and payload comparison of fixed-wing and multirotor drones | Fedor, Ira, Jordan, Jack | Manufacturer data sheets; university or government drone guides |
| Our idea is new | List of existing fire-fighting and grass-mapping drones, and what they don't do | Fedor, Ira, Jordan, Jack | Company sites; news; patents |
| Where fires happen near our grass | Fire and location data for places where these grasses grow | Not assigned (optional) | NASA FIRMS (satellite fire map); Global Forest Watch |

**Source check for every source:** who wrote it (government, university, conservation group, scientific journal), when, and what it proves in one line. Prefer .gov, .edu, .org and journals. Keep YouTube only as extra.

**Best sources are people.** Replies from Bob Leigh, Matthew Lusty, the ROM and the firefighter count as sources too. Record who said what and the date.
