**1. What do you think of our solution?**

> I think your idea is a very good approach. The rainforest is huge, what you need is data that will help you focus on the problem areas. Satellite data will give you the right locations to focus on, and then drones can target specific areas. One challenge I see is getting the drones to those locations. They could be very far from any roads or villages. How will you launch the drones and from where? Can they travel far enough?

**2. What is an autonomous system, and could it benefit our solution?**

> An autonomous system makes decisions without human intervention. The level of decision-making the autonomous system can handle depends on its design and intent. Some systems are designed to be nearly 100% autonomous; others can only work in limited areas. Autonomous systems that work in complex environments, or where there are unpredictable events, need more training (the effort to teach the autonomous system what it should do) and more supervision by humans. When a human makes the decisions on how an autonomous system should act, that is called Human-in-the-Loop.
>
> It is easier to build autonomous systems that fly than those that drive. There are fewer obstacles. For your application, you may want a semi-autonomous drone. That is one that has limited decision-making ability and operates with direct supervision. This makes it much cheaper and easier to build as the human can make all the complex decisions that would confuse the autonomous system. You can have the drones fly over target areas and identify hotspots autonomously, but then a human could review the drone video footage and other data to determine where to drop the water or fire retardant.

**3. What sensors could our drone use or need?**

> Although satellite images are good, they don't always have great detail and they only pass overhead on a schedule you can't control. I suggest you use the satellite data to find the grassy areas, and use drones in those areas to find the hot spots. To do this, your drone could use both LIDAR and heat senors. LIDAR can determine exactly where the grass is versus forest cover. The heat sensors, or infrared camera, will show where the fires are, even if they are very small. This drone that does the data collection can be light and fast. You may want a different drone to drop the water; this one could be bigger and slower so it can lift the water's weight. The two drones could work together.
>
> Here is some more information on how LIDAR is used to map the surface and identify vegetation:
>
> - https://www.sanparks.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/4_Peter-Boucher.pdf
> - https://natural-resources.canada.ca/forests-forestry/sustainable-forest-management/airborne-lidar-forest-inventory-monitoring
> - https://candrone.com/blogs/news/lidar-in-the-leaf-canopy-getting-clean-data-in-dense-forests
